# Sales Trends

<img width="2396" height="886" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/1742eecc-1347-484c-91bc-70e72fb50bd8" />

| Year | Sales | AOV | Order Count |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2019 | $3,868,907.48 | $229.64 | 16,850 |
| 2020 | $10,158,439.46 | $300.16 | 33,851 |
| 2021 | $9,127,894.44 | $254.71 | 35,858 |
| 2022 | $4,957,579.85 | $229.91 | 21,565 |

Sales grew aggressively from 2019 until 2020, before dropping gradually in 2021, at the heart of Covid-19, and then aggressively falling in 2022. Customer behavior changed initially by making smaller orders, but ordering just as often, in 2021, followed by an aggressive drop in orders in 2022, despite dollar amounts returning to prior years. Average Order Value (AOV) remained relatively flat during that period, varying by only $105.27 across the four years, and order counts continued to grow throughout 2021 before dropping by nearly 40% in 2022.

### NA-Specific Sales Drops

Looking at NA-only sales, the top three products (27in 4K Gaming Monitor, Apple Airpods Headphones, and Macbook Air Laptop) make up 86% of all sales. When ThinkPad Laptops are included, those four products account for 96% of total NA sales.

By the end of 2020 all products are doing well into the first large drop in sales; with Apple Airpods Headphones and Macbook Air Laptop nearly tied for second place in sales. By February of 2021, sales for all four products have dropped by **49%**, with only the 27in 4K Gaming Monitor returning close to its original high of $189,399.24 in sales. The Macbook Air Laptop in particular fails to recover, taking a full year to reach only 65% of the prior high.

By the end of 2021, the highest sales month is only 81% of the prior year, with only the 27in 4K Gaming Monitor bouncing back (with the 2021 high point being 98% of last year's high point). This is when the next large drop in sales occurs, and sales again drop by **51%**. All products follow the same trajectory as last year, except for the ThinkPad Laptops, which loses sales over five months instead of the standard two months. Macbook Air Laptops, still struggling more than other products to recover from the prior drop in sales, sheds half (48%) of its total sales again. The aftermath of this drop is very different; no products recover over the following year, with only Apple Airpods Headphones recovering the most with a high point 60% of it's high point last year...


# Monthly and Yearly Growth Rates

<img width="3017" height="1055" alt="image" src="https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/56b4e5c6-03cd-4cd2-8f28-3834b99ef8ad" />

### Sales Growth Rates

Growth rates follow sales amounts relatively closely. Sales growth rates were quite strong in the first two years, until the end of 2020, growing to 150% of the value since January of 2019. 2021 and 2022 showed a gradual, steady decline from that high point, dropping to only a 50% increase from the original January 2019 value.

### Order Count Growth Rates

Order count was more steady, maintaining its high value throughout 2021. 2022 showed a steady decline, plummeting near the end of the year before bouncing back somewhat by December. All in all, like the sales growth, order counts increased overall by 50% over the four years.

### AOV Growth Rates

Average Order Value (AOV) was more volatile overall but over a narrower band of results. For a time, AOV was up about 40% before dropping by half over just a few months and continued dropping more aggressively than Sales and order count growth. Overall, AOV increased only 15% from 2019-2022.

Loyalty members are serving as a stabilizing force of more secure sales, serving as padding against market volatility.

Non-loyalty members were the majority of sales throughout 2019 and 2020. 2020 in particular, during the height of the Covid pandemic, saw an aggressive increase in sales among non-loyalty members, followed by an aggressive drop at the start of 2021, where non-loyalty member sales dropped 51% from December 2020 until March 2021. It took some time for the loyalty program to be beneficial, but with an inflection point in March of 2021, loyalty members were the larger source of income for a year and a half, until September of 2022.

The change was due to steady growth of loyalty member purchasing habits; at the start it was only 2% of all sales, but reached parity with non-loyalty members at the start of 2021. It wasn't until October of 2022 that purchase amounts of loyalty members began to level out while non-loyalty members began to rise.

### AOV

Average Order Value (AOV) for loyalty members has remained very consistent after the first year, averaging $225 for all four years. Non-loyalty AOV has been higher (averaging $263) but slightly more volatile, spiking in orders during the height of the pandemic until June of 2021 where dropping below the AOV of loyalty members for the next year and a half.
